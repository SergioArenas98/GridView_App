GridView Privacy Policy

Last updated: 20 October 2026

This policy explains what information the GridView Android application ("GridView", "the app") processes, why, for how long, and what choices you have. It covers every version of GridView:

  • version 2.0.0 and later (sections 2 to 3.3); and
  • versions 1.x, up to and including 1.2.1 (section 3.4). They may stay installed on devices, and they may remain available on Google Play while version 2.0.0 is rolled out and for devices that cannot install it.

1. Who is responsible

GridView is an unofficial Formula 1 companion app. It is published on Google Play under the developer name Sejuma by Sergio Arenas del Amo, an individual developer based in Spain, who is the data controller for the processing described here.

Contact: gridviewapp1@gmail.com

2. Summary

  • GridView has no user accounts and never asks for your name, email address, phone number, contacts, photos, files or precise location.
  • From version 2.0.0, GridView shows no advertising and contains no advertising or marketing SDK. Versions 1.x showed advertising banners from Google AdMob (section 3.4).
  • From version 2.0.0, GridView does not track which screens you open or what you tap. It records only when a session starts (section 3.3). In versions 1.x, the advertising SDK collects app launches, taps and ad views (section 3.4).
  • Your language and theme choices and the Formula 1 data the app downloads are stored on your device.
  • To fix crashes and keep the app fast, GridView sends crash reports and performance measurements to Google's Firebase services. These are linked to a random installation identifier, not to you.
  • To download Formula 1 data, the app contacts the GridView API, which runs on Cloudflare.

3. Information processed

3.1 Stored only on your device

WhatWhy
Your language and theme choicesTo show the app the way you chose
Formula 1 data downloaded from the GridView API (calendar, results, standings, drivers, teams, circuits)To show it quickly and when you are offline

This information is not sent to us. When you update from GridView 1.x, the app reads the language and theme choices you made in the old version, keeps them, and then deletes the old version's settings and cache.

If Android backup is turned on for your device, Android may include this app data in your device backup with Google. That backup is controlled by your device and Google account settings, not by GridView.

3.2 Requests to the GridView API

When the app downloads Formula 1 data, your device connects to the GridView API over an encrypted connection (HTTPS). The API is hosted by Cloudflare, which necessarily receives your device's IP address and standard request details (time, requested resource and a random request number generated for each request). Requests contain no account, device identifier or personal information.

We use this only to deliver the data and to keep the service working and secure. We do not use it to identify you or to build a profile, and any operational logs are short-lived.

3.3 Crash reports and performance measurements (Firebase)

GridView uses Firebase Crashlytics and Firebase Performance Monitoring, provided by Google, together with the Firebase components they need (Installations, Sessions and Remote Config). They process:

  • Crash and error reports: the stack trace and the state of the app when the problem happened.
    • For a crash, the report also carries the error's own type and description, which the app or Android generated. That description can contain technical values from the moment of the crash.
    • GridView adds only a small set of fixed labels, such as which feature was affected. It never adds your preferences' values, the content you viewed or web addresses to these labels.
  • Performance measurements: for example app start time, the time taken by the app's own operations, and memory and processor use.
  • Session information: when the app comes to the foreground to start a session. Firebase Sessions uses it to group crash and performance data.
  • Device and app information: device model and manufacturer, Android version, app version, language, country, time zone, available memory and storage, and network type.
  • Random installation identifiers: a Firebase installation ID and a Crashlytics installation identifier. They identify an installation of the app, not a person, and are replaced when you clear the app's data.
  • IP address: Firebase Performance Monitoring uses the IP address to work out which country a measurement comes from.

We use this information only to find and fix crashes and to improve performance.

Retention, as published by Firebase: Crashlytics data is kept for 90 days. Performance Monitoring keeps installation-linked performance data for 60 days and IP-linked events for 30 days. Firebase installation identifiers are kept until they are deleted.

3.4 GridView 1.x (version 1.2.1 and earlier)

Versions 1.x work differently from version 2.0.0. They contain no Firebase Crashlytics or Performance Monitoring, and section 3.3 does not apply to them. Instead:

  • Advertising (Google AdMob). Versions 1.x show advertising banners through the Google Mobile Ads SDK, which starts when the app opens. Google collects and receives, through that SDK:
    • your device's IP address, from which an approximate location can be derived;
    • app interactions such as app launches, taps and ad views;
    • diagnostic information such as app launch time and performance;
    • device identifiers, including the Android advertising ID and app set ID.

    Google uses this information for advertising (including, where your settings allow, personalised ads), analytics and fraud prevention, under its own policies: https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites. Versions 1.x do not show a consent message before ads are requested.

  • Unity Ads SDK. Versions 1.x also include the Unity Ads SDK. The app's own code never starts it.
  • The 1.x data server. Versions 1.x downloaded Formula 1 data from a separate GridView server hosted on Railway, which received your device's IP address with each request. That server has been shut down and no longer receives requests.
  • On the device. Versions 1.x keep your settings and a cache of Formula 1 data on your device. Updating to version 2.0.0 keeps your language and theme choices and deletes the rest (section 3.1).

Your choices for versions 1.x:

  • Update to version 2.0.0 or later, which contains no advertising. It needs Android 7.0 or later.
  • Reset or delete your advertising ID, or opt out of ad personalisation, in Android settings. Look for Privacy → Ads or Google → Ads, depending on your device.
  • Uninstall the app.

4. Legal basis

For users in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, we rely on our legitimate interests (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR):

  • in delivering the Formula 1 data you ask the app to show (section 3.2); and
  • in keeping the app stable and fast (section 3.3).

We have limited this processing to what those purposes need, as described above.

The advertising in versions 1.x (section 3.4) is processed by Google for its own purposes, as described in Google's policies. Versions 1.x did not ask for consent to it. Version 2.0.0 removes it.

5. Who receives the information

We do not sell or rent your information. From version 2.0.0 we do not share it for advertising or marketing. In versions 1.x, the advertising SDK sends the information listed in section 3.4 to Google, which uses it for its own advertising purposes: https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites.

Information is processed on our behalf by these service providers, under their data-processing terms:

  • Google (Firebase Crashlytics and Performance Monitoring): https://firebase.google.com/support/privacy
  • Cloudflare (hosting of the GridView API): https://www.cloudflare.com/privacypolicy/

These providers may process information outside your country, including in the United States, under safeguards such as the European Commission's standard contractual clauses or the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework.

We may also disclose information if the law requires it.

6. Your choices and rights

  • Stop all processing: uninstall the app. Without the app, nothing is sent.
  • Reset the identifiers: clear the app's data in Android settings. This gives the installation new random identifiers.
  • Your rights: depending on where you live, you can ask for access to, correction or deletion of your information, or object to or restrict its processing. Write to gridviewapp1@gmail.com. Because GridView does not know who you are, we may need your help to find the information concerned (for example, the approximate date and time of a crash).
  • Complaints: you can complain to a data protection authority. In Spain, this is the Agencia Española de Protección de Datos (https://www.aepd.es).

7. Children

GridView is a general-audience sports app. It is not directed at children, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from children.
